# National Security Archive (GWU) sources

Declassified U.S. government documents (State/Embassy cables, CIA/DIA reports, NSC and OSD memos) and translated Soviet records published in the National Security Archive's open **Electronic Briefing Books** (EBBs). The subscription *Digital National Security Archive* (ProQuest) was not used; everything below is freely downloadable from `nsarchive.gwu.edu` / `nsarchive2.gwu.edu`.

Survey scope: 26 briefing books (31 web pages), 623 PDFs downloaded. Each PDF was rated for relevance to the AF/Pak jihadist-network use case from the briefing book's own document descriptions (high 350, medium 149, low 69, none 55); high/medium documents were ingested. Low/none (FBI domestic terrorism, nuclear policy, non-AF/Pak terrorism, US bureaucratic history) were skipped.

**Ingested: 446 documents, 4335 pages (2369 pages OCR'd with Tesseract; the rest had text layers).**

## Caveats

- 15 high/medium-rated documents were dropped after extraction because their content was outside the AF/Pak network scope (global *Patterns of Terrorism* surveys, FBI domestic-terrorism reports, the 740-page *Virus A* Soviet memoir, DoJ/CIA OIG inspection reports, Yemen/Caucasus threat papers, Rumsfeld detainee/interrogation memos).
- `allied_with`, `rival_of` and some `family` edges are also derived from the extractor's per-entity `allies`/`rivals`/`family` fields and carry no supporting quote; treat them as weak unless a quoted edge of the same type exists.
- Most documents are scanned cables OCR'd with Tesseract; names are often garbled (`UBL`, `USAMA BIN LADIN`, `HEKMATYAR`/`HIKMATYAR`, all-caps cable text). Transliteration variants were added to `scripts/aliases.json` where they could be resolved confidently; review `review_docs.py` output before trusting a low-frequency name.
- Redactions appear as gaps; relationships are only extracted where a quote survives.
- Document descriptions, dates and originators in the headers come from the briefing books' own document lists; where the page did not describe a PDF, the title falls back to the filename.
- Cable-era U.S. officials (ambassadors, desk officers) appear as persons because they are actors in meetings with Taliban/Pakistani officials; use `entities.n_documents`/sources to filter.
