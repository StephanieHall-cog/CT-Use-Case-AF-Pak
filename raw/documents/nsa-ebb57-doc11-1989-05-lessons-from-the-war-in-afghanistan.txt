(0) Many Mujahideen strengths were common to most insurgent

movements. These included:

- Pamiliarity with the terrain

- Tactical mobility

unusually rugged and highly motivated fighters. This motivation
stemmed from the historic dislike for foreign occupiers, az weli

Mujahideen Weaknesses
(U0) The most glaring weaknesses in the insurgency were:
- No unified leadership or strategic planning
- Sporadic outbreaks of intergroup violence (which

- Limited firepower

- Difficult logistics

- Limited communications

Soviet Objectives  .

(U0) Rather than being an ‘invasion,’ the introduction of
Soviet combat forces in December 1979 was an ‘intervention’ in e
COIN effort which had begun shortly after the Afghan Communist
Party coup in April 1978. The USSR sent its forces into
Afghanistan primarily for the purpose of taking over security
responsibilities, so that government forces could concentrate on
putting down the ever-growing insurgency. Thus the initial
Soviet objectives were to:

- Control the cities and towns
- Secure the major lines of communications (LOC)
- Train and equip government forces

(U) As the insurgency expanded and the Afghan Army proved
inadequate to the task, the Soviet “Limited Contingent’ in
Afghanistan soon found itself enmeshed in full-fledged COIN
operations. Thus, by mid-1980, the Soviets acquired the
following additional migsions: :

~ Eliminate inaurgent centers ;
- Separate insurgents from the population
- Deny by interdiction outside aid and sanctuary
Nonetheless, this ‘low-intensity conflict’ was to be conducted
with e minimum of logseg in personnel and equipment. As pointed
out above, there would be no gignificant troop buildup, nor great
expansion of the logistics and transportation infrastructures.
The Soviets partially succeeded in only the first three of the
above missions.
